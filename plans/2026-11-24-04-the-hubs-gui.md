# plans/2026-11-24-04-the-hubs-gui.md

The hub's GUI and who may call the hub, the Void way throughout: Void pages with shadcn, and Better
Auth for every kind of caller. Agreed with the owner (2026-11-24), from first principles after the
hub became the project. Nothing here is built yet.

## Agreed

- **The Void way, and nothing brought in beside it.** The hub's screens are Void pages in the hub
  app (`pages/`, loaders, page actions, `@void/react`), built from shadcn components with
  Tailwind, as the example's two apps are. No web component, no script the hub serves for other
  apps to load, no token scheme of our own.
- **No package to publish.** Nobody installs anything of ours to use the hub, and we publish
  nothing to npm:
  - **the GUI in an app is an iframe** of a page the hub serves. The app adds one route of its
    own that passes requests on to the hub (over its binding, or to its address), and points the
    iframe at that route: so the app's own sign-in guards it, the page is on the app's own domain
    (no cross-site cookies, no token in an address), and the hub renders all of it. It looks like
    the hub, with light or dark and a few colours passed in;
  - **publishing an event is one request** to the hub, with the Worker's key;
  - **following a stream can be one route**: the hub reads for the subscriber and sends it each
    event, and a 200 acknowledges it (anything else, and it's sent again), with a secret per
    subscription so the Worker knows it's the hub's. The hub's own, on top of the protocol's
    subscriptions, which stay as they are for whoever wants signed wakes;
  - `client.ts` and `bus.ts` stay in the repo for the example and for copying; the protocol's
    authors publish `@durable-streams/client` for anyone who wants the whole protocol.
    A shadcn registry (components copied into an app, to look like the app) only later, if an
    app needs that: it puts the GUI's code back in each app, which is what this avoids.
- **Better Auth through and through** (it's Void's auth, `void/auth`, with its plugins in
  `auth.ts`). Everything about who is calling the hub and what they may do is Better Auth's:
  - people on the console sign in with it; the admin plugin says who is an admin;
  - a Worker sends an API key the hub issued (the API-key plugin, `@better-auth/api-key`): one
    per Worker, with an expiry, a rate limit, and revoking, made in the console;
  - what a person or a key may do (read a stream, write one, manage subscriptions) is its
    access-control plugin's permissions.
    `HUB_TOKEN` and the hub's own token check (`middleware/01.workers.ts`) go.
- **Organizations from the start** (Better Auth's organization plugin), so one hub serves one
  project or many. A new hub makes one organization by itself; someone with one project never
  meets the word. Streams, subscriptions and keys each belong to an organization; a Worker still
  writes to `orders/paid`, and its key says whose.
- **One hub is one Void app with one D1,** as Void makes it. Where the database is, and its
  replicas, are Cloudflare's and Void's to decide (`database.readReplication`), not ours.
  Organizations in a hub share its database. A project that needs its own gets its own hub:
  another deploy of the same app.
- **Each app keeps its own users.** The shop's Better Auth is the shop's; the hub doesn't know its
  users. An app decides who may see which stream and reads the hub with its key.

## Outside Better Auth, on purpose

- A subscriber's answer to a wake carries the wake's own token, and wakes are signed with the
  hub's key: the Durable Streams protocol's, and what its conformance suite checks.
- Storing and delivering streams.

## Ours to get right, since nobody else will

- **Organizations kept apart:** one limit in the stream store that every query goes through, and
  tests that try to cross from one organization to another in every operation.
- **The database not filling:** old stream data expires (the protocol's `Stream-TTL`).
- **One caller not swamping the rest:** a rate limit per key.
- **A key checked without a database read on every request:** the API-key plugin's own secondary
  store and deferred updates; measured, not assumed.
- **A first run that needs no thought:** `vp run deploy` makes the first admin, the first
  organization and a first key, and prints the key once.

## To build, in order

1. Better Auth in the hub: `auth.ts` with the admin, organization, access-control and API-key
   plugins; the protocol's routes checked by key; the conformance tests sending one.
2. Organizations in the stream store, the subscriptions and the directory, with the tests of
   their separation.
3. One envelope for published events (`bus.ts`): its id, type and data, the id of the event that
   caused it, and who it concerns. What lets a chain across Workers be shown.
4. Listing (the protocol has none): an organization's streams, and its subscriptions with how far
   each has acknowledged and the state of its wake.
5. The console: sign-in; streams, with one tailed live; subscriptions and who is behind; the map
   of who follows what; one event's chain; keys and members.
6. Delivery by one route: a subscription that has the hub send each event to the subscriber's
   address, acknowledged by its answer.
7. The views for apps: hub pages made to be shown in an iframe, and the one route an app adds to
   pass them through. Then the example uses them: out go its pane, its `/streams` routes, the
   middleware fetching activity, the per-viewer activity streams and the reporting wrappers
   (`examples/activity` shrinks to nearly nothing), and its two shared secrets (`HUB_TOKEN`,
   `AFFILIATES_HOST_TOKEN`) become keys.

Also wanted by anyone outside this repo, and not part of this plan: the Deploy to Cloudflare
button.

## Checked against the packages (2026-11-24)

- Void: a root `auth.ts` (`defineAuth`) takes Better Auth's plugins, and Void generates their
  tables and migrations (`void db generate`).
- `@better-auth/api-key` 1.7.27: a key belongs to a user or to an organization; carries
  permissions (checked when the key is); has a rate limit, an expiry, a quota that refills,
  metadata; can count as a session, so Void's `getUser` and `requireAuth` hold for a Worker's
  request as for a person's; keys can be kept in a fast secondary store, and their counters
  updated after the answer. So no cache of our own for checking keys.
- Not checked yet: whether Void gives Better Auth a secondary store on Cloudflare, or we supply
  one. To find out at step 1.
- In the example, not Better Auth today: the service's shared `AFFILIATES_HOST_TOKEN`, and the
  service asking the shop who's signed in by passing the browser's cookies on at every request
  (Better Auth's JWT plugin: the shop signs, the service checks it itself).

## Later, if wanted

Better Auth's device authorization (a local Worker or a command-line tool gets its key by being
approved in the browser), sign-in with GitHub or Google, two-factor for admins, and its Stripe
plugin for billing by organization.

## What is given up

- What a Worker does between taking an event and acknowledging it (a queue hop, an email) shows
  only if it publishes it.
- The hub is more than a protocol server: accounts, keys, organizations, listing and pages are
  its own, beside the protocol. Each is said as such in the README.

## Done (2026-11-24)

- **1. Better Auth in the hub:** `auth.ts` (admin, organization, API key); `HUB_TOKEN` gone;
  `vp run key`; the example's two apps a key each.
- **2. Organizations in the streams:** by address (`src/streams.ts`): each organization's
  streams, subscriptions and directory under a root of its own, `/v1/o/<organization>/stream`,
  which a Worker's `/v1/stream` is turned into by its key. A wake is answered at the address a
  Worker knows, with the wake's token, tried in each organization (it holds in one).
  `test/organizations.test.ts`.
- **3. The envelope:** `bus.ts` events carry `causedBy` (the event a follower was dealing with
  when it published) and `at`.
- **4. Listing:** `src/listing.ts`, for the console (not a route of its own yet).
- **5. The console:** `pages/index` (sign in; an organization's streams, followers, keys) and
  `pages/stream` (one stream, live). Looked at in a browser. No tests of its pages yet.
- **6. Delivery by one route:** `PUT /v1/push/<id>` (`src/push.ts`): underneath, a webhook
  subscription whose wake the hub takes in process, reads for, and sends on.

Both suites after all of it: the hub's 348 of 348 (the protocol's 344, and 4 of its own), the
example's 31 of 31.
